Data-Processing Inequality

14 July 2021Hao Wu~3 min read
On this page
Reference

Suppose that XXX be a random variable.

  • We obtain a new random variable YYY by applying a stochastic mapping fff on XXX, namely, Y=f(X)Y = f(X)Y=f(X).
  • We further post-process YYY by another stochastic mapping ggg to obtain ZZZ, i.e., Z=g(Y)Z = g(Y)Z=g(Y).

The function ggg depends only on YYY and is independent of XXX. Therefore, the XXX and YYY are conditionally independent given YYY. The random variables XXX, YYY, ZZZ are said to form a Markov chain, denoted by

X→Y→Z. X \rightarrow Y \rightarrow Z. X→Y→Z.

The data processing inequality states that postprocessing can not increases the correlations.

Theorem (Data Processing Inequality). If the random variables X,YX, YX,Y and ZZZ form a Markov chain: X→Y→ZX \rightarrow Y \rightarrow ZX→Y→Z. Then

I(X;Y)≥I(X;Z), I(X;Y)≥I(X;Y∣Z). I( X; Y) \ge I(X ; Z),\,\\ I( X; Y) \ge I(X; Y \mid Z). I(X;Y)≥I(X;Z),I(X;Y)≥I(X;Y∣Z).

Proof. By chain rule of mutual information, we expand the mutual information in two different ways:

I(X;Y,Z)=I(X;Z)+I(X;Y∣Z)=I(X;Y)+I(X;Z∣Y). \begin{aligned} I(X; Y, Z) &= I(X ; Z) + I( X; Y \mid Z) \\ &= I(X ; Y) + I( X; Z \mid Y). \end{aligned} I(X;Y,Z)​=I(X;Z)+I(X;Y∣Z)=I(X;Y)+I(X;Z∣Y).​

Since XXX and ZZZ are conditionally independent through YYY, it holds that

I(X;Z∣Y)=0. I(X; Z \mid Y) = 0. I(X;Z∣Y)=0.

Therefore,

I(X;Y)=I(X;Z)+I(X;Y∣Z). I(X; Y) = I(X; Z) + I(X; Y \mid Z). I(X;Y)=I(X;Z)+I(X;Y∣Z).

The inequalities follow form non-negativity of mutual information.

■\blacksquare■
