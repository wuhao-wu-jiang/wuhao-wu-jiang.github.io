Problem

Suppose we have a set of facilities FFF and a set of clients CCC, and would like to open a subset of facilities to serve the clients. The cost consists of two parts:

  1. one for opening the selected facilities;
  2. one for connecting each client to its nearest facility.

Formally, denote fif_ifi​ the cost of facility i∈Fi \in Fi∈F and du,id_{u,i}du,i​ the distance from a client u∈Cu \in Cu∈C to a facility i∈Fi \in Fi∈F. Further, we assume that the distances satisfy the following constraint:

dv,i≤dv,j+du,j+du,i,∀u,v∈C,i,j∈F. d_{v, i} \le d_{v, j} + d_{u, j} + d_{u, i}, \qquad \forall u, v \in C,\quad i, j \in F. dv,i​≤dv,j​+du,j​+du,i​,∀u,v∈C,i,j∈F.

Let S⊆FS \subseteq FS⊆F be a subset of facilities. The cost of SSS is given by

∑i∈Sfi+∑u∈Cmin⁡i∈Sdu,i \sum_{i \in S} f_i + \sum_{u \in C} \min_{i \in S} d_{u,i} i∈S∑​fi​+u∈C∑​i∈Smin​du,i​

The problem is to find a set SSS that minimizes this cost.

Intuitive Explanation

Here we give a more intuitive way of deriving the dual. First, observe that ∑u∈Cmin⁡i∈Fdu,i\sum_{u \in C} \min_{i \in F} d_{u,i}∑u∈C​mini∈F​du,i​ is a trivial lower bound of the primal programming. In this case, each client is assigned to its nearest facility. However we ignore facility cost entirely.

To incorporate the facility cost, for each facility iii we divide its cost fif_ifi​ among the clients, such that client uuu needs to pay βu,i\beta_{u, i}βu,i​ if it is assigned facility iii. Note that βu,i\beta_{u, i}βu,i​ can be arbitrary non-negative number as long as it satisfies that ∑u∈Cβu,i=fi\sum_{u \in C} \beta_{u, i} = f_i∑u∈C​βu,i​=fi​ for all i∈Fi \in Fi∈F.

How happy are we with the 444-approximation algorithm? Probably not. In the previous example, when a facility is opened, only one client contributes to its opening cost. However, in the primal solution, many other clients assigned to it do not contribute to the opening cost, which implies that the current analysis may not be tight.

Randomized Rounding [3]

333-Approximation Rounding

In the section, we improve the approximation ratio of 444 to an expected value of 333. In the previous rounding, we bound the opening cost as

∑u∈Bmin⁡i∈N(u)fi≤OPT \sum_{u \in B} \min_{i \in N(u) } f_i \le OPT u∈B∑​i∈N(u)min​fi​≤OPT
