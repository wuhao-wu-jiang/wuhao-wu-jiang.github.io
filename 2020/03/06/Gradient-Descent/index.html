Problem

Gradient descent is a greedy search method for optimization, whose analysis varies depending on the kind of constraints you place on it.

We study a baby version of the method. Let fff be a twice continuous differentiable and convex function. We are interested in the minimization problem:

min⁡x∈Rnf(x) \min_{x \in \mathbb{R }^n} f(x) x∈Rnmin​f(x)

The convexity of fff guarantees the existence of a unique minimum value min⁡x∈Rnf(x)\min_{x \in \mathbb{R }^n} f(x)minx∈Rn​f(x).

In addition, we assume that fff indeed achieves this minimum value at some point, denoted as x∗x^*x∗. We need this assumption, as in some case, fff can never achieve the minimum value. For example, let f(x)=exf(x) = e^xf(x)=ex. fff approaches 000 when x→−∞x \rightarrow -\inftyx→−∞ but never achieves it.

Convergence

To analyze the convergence rate of fff, we need additional assumptions. Here we adopt two strong ones:

Assumptions.

  1. fff is β\betaβ smooth, i.e., ∇f(z)≤βI\nabla f(z) \le \beta I∇f(z)≤βI for ∀z∈Rn\forall z \in \mathbb{R }^n∀z∈Rn.
  2. fff is α\alphaα strongly convex, i.e., ∇f(z)≥αI\nabla f(z) \ge \alpha I∇f(z)≥αI for ∀z∈Rn\forall z \in \mathbb{R }^n∀z∈Rn.

β\betaβ upper bounds the largest possible eigenvalue of the Hessian of fff at each point in R\mathbb{R}R, and α\alphaα lower bounds it. By definition we have β≥α\beta \ge \alphaβ≥α.

  1. For each t∈[T]t \in [T]t∈[T], let Δt≐f(xt)−f(x∗)\Delta_t \doteq f(x^t) - f(x^*)Δt​≐f(xt)−f(x∗) be the gap between f(xt)f(x^t)f(xt) and the minimum value f(x∗)f(x^*)f(x∗).
