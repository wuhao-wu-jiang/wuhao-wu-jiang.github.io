Integration of Real-valued Functions

Motivation

Given an interval [a,b][a, b][a,b] and some function f:[a,b]→Rf: [a, b] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}f:[a,b]→R, we want to create some rule, that computes the area under the curve y=f(x)y = f(x)y=f(x) between x=ax = ax=a and x=bx = bx=b.

This rule, which assigns real numbers to functions, can be itself represented as a function φ[a,b]:F([a,b])→R\varphi_{[a, b]} : \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}φ[a,b]​:F([a,b])→R. Here, F([a,b])\mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)F([a,b]), the domain of φ\varphiφ, refers to a collection of functions defined on [a,b][a, b][a,b] such that the area under the curve y=f(x)y = f(x)y=f(x) between x=ax = ax=a and x=bx = bx=b is well-defined according to the rule.

The integral of a function f∈F([a,b])f \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)f∈F([a,b]) is defined as the value obtained under the rule φ[a,b]\varphi_{[a, b]}φ[a,b]​. To represent this value, we conventionally use the notation:

∫abf dx≐φ[a,b](f). \int_a^b f \, dx \doteq \varphi_{[a, b]} (f). ∫ab​fdx≐φ[a,b]​(f).

Similarly, when considering another interval [s,t][s, t][s,t], we can establish a similar rule φ[s,t]\varphi_{[s, t]}φ[s,t]​ with domain F([s,t])\mathcal{F} \big( [s, t] \big)F([s,t]). The integral of fff over [s,t][s, t][s,t] is denoted as:

∫stf dx≐φ[s,t](f). \int_s^t f \, dx \doteq \varphi_{[s, t]} (f). ∫st​fdx≐φ[s,t]​(f).

In the subsequent discussion, we will employ the notation on the left-hand side exclusively.

The integral should adhere to our geometric understanding of areas and satisfy various properties. First and foremost, the integral should accurately compute the area of rectangles.

Property (Area of Rectangle). If ∃c∈R\exists c \in \mathbb{R}∃c∈R, s.t., f(x)=cf(x) = cf(x)=c for each x∈[a,b]x \in [a, b]x∈[a,b], then f∈F([a,b])f \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)f∈F([a,b]) and ∫abf dx=c⋅(b−a)\int_a^b f \, dx = c \cdot (b - a)∫ab​fdx=c⋅(b−a). .

Secondly, the integrals should demonstrate linearity.

Property (Linearity).

  1. If f∈F([a,b])f \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)f∈F([a,b]) and c∈Rc \in \mathbb{R}c∈R, then c⋅f∈F([a,b])c \cdot f \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)c⋅f∈F([a,b]) and
∫abc⋅f dx=c⋅∫abf dx. \int _{a}^{b} c \cdot f \, dx = c \cdot \int_{a}^{b} f \, dx. ∫ab​c⋅fdx=c⋅∫ab​fdx.
  1. If f1,f2∈F([a,b])f_1, f_2 \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)f1​,f2​∈F([a,b]), then f1+f2∈F([a,b])f_1 + f_2 \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)f1​+f2​∈F([a,b]), and
∫abf1+f2 dx=∫abf1 dx+∫abf2 dx. \int _{a}^{b} f_1 + f_2 \, dx = \int_{a}^{b} f_1 \, dx + \int_{a}^{b} f_2 \, dx. ∫ab​f1​+f2​dx=∫ab​f1​dx+∫ab​f2​dx.
  1. If f∈F([a,b])f \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)f∈F([a,b]) and c∈(a,b)c \in (a, b)c∈(a,b), then f∈F([a,c])f \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, c] \big)f∈F([a,c]) and f∈F([c,b])f \in \mathcal{F} \big( [c, b] \big)f∈F([c,b]), and
∫abf dx=∫acf dx+∫cbf dx. \int _{a}^{b} f \, dx = \int _{a}^{c} f \, dx + \int _{c}^{b} f \, dx. ∫ab​fdx=∫ac​fdx+∫cb​fdx.

The integrals should allow for meaningful comparisons.

Property (Monotonicity).

  1. If f1,f2∈F([a,b])f_1, f_2 \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)f1​,f2​∈F([a,b]) and f1≤f2f_1 \le f_2f1​≤f2​, then
∫abf1 dx≤∫abf2 dx. \int_{a}^{b} f_1 \, dx \le \int_{a}^{b} f_2 \, dx. ∫ab​f1​dx≤∫ab​f2​dx.
  1. If f∈F([a,b])f \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)f∈F([a,b]) and ∣f∣≤M|f| \le M∣f∣≤M, then
∣∫abf dx∣≤M⋅(b−a). \left| \int_{a}^{b} f \, dx \right| \le M \cdot (b - a). ​∫ab​fdx​≤M⋅(b−a).

So far, it is straightforward to specify a family of functions, and a rule that assigning real numbers to them,satisfying the aforementioned properties. For instance, we can define F([a,b])\mathcal{F}\big( [a, b] \big)F([a,b]) as the set of step functions on [a,b][a, b][a,b]. Each step function fff can be expressed as:

f(x)=∑i∈[n]ci⋅IEi(x), f(x) = \sum_{i \in [n]} c_i \cdot \mathbb{I}_{E_i} (x), f(x)=i∈[n]∑​ci​⋅IEi​​(x),

where Ei,i∈[n]E_i, i \in [n]Ei​,i∈[n], are sub-intervals, forming a partition of [a,b][a, b][a,b]. Further, we can define the integral as

∫abf dx≐∑i∈[n]ci⋅∣Ei∣, \int_a^b f \, dx \doteq \sum_{i \in [n]} c_i \cdot |E_i|, ∫ab​fdx≐i∈[n]∑​ci​⋅∣Ei​∣,

where ∣Ei∣|E_i|∣Ei​∣ is the length of EiE_iEi​.

However, it is also desirable for the constructed integrals to satisfy convergence properties.

Property (Convergence).

  1. If fi∈F([a,b])f_i \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)fi​∈F([a,b]) for each i∈N+i \in \mathbb{N}^+i∈N+ and fi→ff_i \rightarrow ffi​→f under some metric, then f∈F([a,b])f \in \mathcal{F} \big( [a, b] \big)f∈F([a,b]) and
lim⁡n→∞∫abfi dx=∫abf dx. \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \int_{a}^{b} f_i \, dx = \int_{a}^{b} f \, dx. n→∞lim​∫ab​fi​dx=∫ab​fdx.

Unfortunately, the previous construction does not satisfy convergence properties. Additional considerations and refinements are required in order to establish an integral that fulfills the desired convergence properties.

Construction

Bernhard Riemann presented the following construction of integral.

Let [a,b][a, b][a,b] be a given interval. P={x0,x1,…,xn}P = \{ x_0 , x_1 , \ldots, x_n \}P={x0​,x1​,…,xn​} is a partition of [a,b][a, b][a,b], if

a=x0≤x1⋯≤xn=b. a = x_0 \le x_1 \cdots \le x_n = b. a=x0​≤x1​⋯≤xn​=b.

For each i∈[n]i \in [n]i∈[n], let Δxi≐xi,−xi−1\Delta x_i \doteq x_i, - x_{i - 1}Δxi​≐xi​,−xi−1​.

Assume f:[a,b]→Rf: [a, b] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}f:[a,b]→R is a bounded function. Associated with each partition PPP, define

Mi≐sup⁡x∈[xi−1,xi]f(x),mi≐inf⁡x∈[xi−1,xi]f(x). M_i \doteq \sup_{x \in [x_{i - 1}, x_i]} f(x), \qquad m_i \doteq \inf_{x \in [x_{i - 1}, x_i]} f(x). Mi​≐x∈[xi−1​,xi​]sup​f(x),mi​≐x∈[xi−1​,xi​]inf​f(x).

Further, let

U(P,f)≐∑i∈[n]Mi⋅Δxi,L(P,f)≐∑i∈[n]mi⋅Δxi. U(P, f) \doteq \sum_{i \in [n]} M_i \cdot \Delta x_i, \quad L(P, f) \doteq \sum_{i \in [n]} m_i \cdot \Delta x_i. U(P,f)≐i∈[n]∑​Mi​⋅Δxi​,L(P,f)≐i∈[n]∑​mi​⋅Δxi​.

Finally, consider

∫ab‾f dx≐inf⁡U(P,f),∫ab‾f dx≐sup⁡L(P,f), \overline{\int _{a}^{b}} f \, dx \doteq \inf U(P, f), \quad \underline{\int_{a}^{b}} f \, dx \doteq \sup L(P, f), ∫ab​​fdx≐infU(P,f),∫ab​​fdx≐supL(P,f),

where the inf and sup are taken over all partitions PPP of [a,b][a, b][a,b].

Definition f:[a,b]→Rf: [a, b] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}f:[a,b]→R is considered Riemann integrable, if

∫ab‾f dx=∫ab‾f dx. \overline{\int _{a}^{b}} f \, dx = \underline{\int_{a}^{b}} f \, dx. ∫ab​​fdx=∫ab​​fdx.

In this cases, we define the integral of fff over the interval [a,b][a, b][a,b] as:

∫abf dx≐∫ab‾f dx=∫ab‾f dx. \int _{a}^{b} f \, dx \doteq \overline{\int _{a}^{b}} f \, dx = \underline{\int_{a}^{b}} f \, dx. ∫ab​fdx≐∫ab​​fdx=∫ab​​fdx.

We denote the collection of functions that are Riemann integrable on [a,b][a, b][a,b] as R([a,b])\mathfrak{R}\big( [a, b] \big)R([a,b]).

Remark. It is reasonable to assume that fff is bounded on the closed interval [a,b][a, b][a,b], in order that it is Riemann integrable. If fff is unbounded, for each partition PPP, there exists some i∈[n]i \in [n]i∈[n], s.t., Mi−mi=∞M_i - m_i = \inftyMi​−mi​=∞. Hence it is impossible that ∫ab‾f dx=∫ab‾f dx\overline{\int _{a}^{b}} f \, dx = \underline{\int_{a}^{b}} f \, dx∫ab​​fdx=∫ab​​fdx.

Properties

It is straightforward to see the proposed construction of integral captures the area of rectangles, and demonstrates linearity and monotonicity.

Assume that for each n∈N+n \in \mathbb{N}^+n∈N+, fn∈R([a,b])f_n \in \mathfrak{R} \big( [a, b] \big)fn​∈R([a,b]), and that fn→ff_n \rightarrow ffn​→f uniformly on [a,b][a, b][a,b] (i.e., sup⁡x∈[a,b]∣fn(x)−f(x)∣→0\sup_{x \in [a, b]} |f_n(x) - f(x)| \rightarrow 0supx∈[a,b]​∣fn​(x)−f(x)∣→0). Existence

In this section, we delve into the functions are encompassed within R([a,b])\mathfrak{R} \big( [a, b] \big)R([a,b]).
